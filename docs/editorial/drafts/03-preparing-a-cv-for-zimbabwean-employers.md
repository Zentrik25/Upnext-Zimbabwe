## What Zimbabwean employers usually look for first

Read a few notices for the kind of post you want and you will see the same requests again and again:

- **School results.** Many posts, including entry-level and artisan posts, ask for "5 O Levels including English
  Language and Mathematics". List your O and A Level subjects and grades clearly, with the examining board (ZIMSEC or
  Cambridge) and the year, so a screener can tick the requirement without searching.
- **Post-school qualifications.** Give the exact title (for example "National Diploma in Electrical Power
  Engineering", "BSc Honours in Accounting"), the institution, the year and, for degrees, the class. If a programme
  asks for "an upper second or better", the class must be visible. [VERIFY: confirm the examining body for national
  certificates and diplomas is HEXCO and how candidates should write it.]
- **Professional registration.** If you are registered or studying with a professional body (for example ACCA, CIMA,
  ICSAZ, or the Engineering Council of Zimbabwe), say which level and give a membership number where you have one.
  [VERIFY: body names editors want to list.]
- **Licences and clearances.** A driver's licence class, a defensive driving certificate, a medical certificate or a
  police clearance is often a stated requirement for drivers, truck assistants and field roles. List those you hold.
- **Software.** Name the tools the listing names (for example Pastel, Sage, QuickBooks, Excel, AutoCAD) and how you
  used them.

## A worked example: an entry-level contract post

A November 2026 notice for a beverage distribution post asked for 5 O Levels including English and Mathematics, one
year of sales or distribution experience, and a police clearance, with applications by e-mail by a stated Friday.
A CV that answers it puts, near the top:

```
Education
ZIMSEC O Level, 2021: 6 subjects including English Language (B) and Mathematics (C)

Experience
Delivery assistant, [Employer], Harare, Jan 2024 – present
- Loaded and checked about 40 drops a day against the delivery note
- Collected cash and recorded it on the route sheet

Clearances
Police clearance, issued [month year]
```

The screener sees every requirement in the first half of the page.

## Personal details

A phone number you answer and an e-mail address you check are enough. You do not need your national ID number, date
of birth, marital status, religion or a photograph unless the listing asks for them; leaving them out also protects
you if the CV is forwarded. [VERIFY: whether any public-sector applications require the ID number on the CV.]

## Referees

Zimbabwean notices often ask for two or three "contactable" referees, sometimes including a current or recent
supervisor. Give name, position, organisation, phone and e-mail, and ask each referee first.

## Sending it

- One PDF unless the listing says otherwise; some employers ask for everything in a single PDF.
- Use the exact subject line the listing gives, including any reference number.
- Postal and hand-delivered applications: print on plain paper, put the post title on the envelope if asked, and keep
  a copy of everything you submit.
