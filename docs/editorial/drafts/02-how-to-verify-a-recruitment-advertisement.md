# How to verify a recruitment advertisement — DRAFT (new guide)

Proposed description: "Six checks, in order, that show whether a vacancy advert in Zimbabwe is genuine before you send
documents or money."

---

Checking an advert takes ten minutes. Do it before you send a CV, an identity document or a certificate, and always
before anything involving money.

## 1. Find the organisation yourself

Search for the organisation's name and open its website or official social media page by typing the address
yourself, not by following a link in the advert or a message. If you cannot find any trace of the organisation, or
the advert does not name one, treat it with caution.

## 2. Find the same vacancy on the organisation's own channel

Large employers publish every post on their own careers page or recruitment portal. Look for the same title,
reference number and closing date. If the organisation lists vacancies but this one is not there, contact it (step 4)
before applying.

Some genuine employers, especially smaller firms, councils and schools, advertise only in the press or by notice.
Absence from a website is a reason to check further, not proof of a fake.

## 3. Compare the contact details

- An e-mail address on the organisation's own domain (for example `name@organisation.co.zw`) is a good sign.
- A free webmail address (Gmail, Yahoo, Outlook) is common for small employers and for some councils, but it is also
  what fake recruiters use. UPNEXT shows a note on listings whose applications go to free webmail.
- A WhatsApp number as the only contact needs extra checking.
- A link shortener (such as `lnkd.in` or `bit.ly`) hides where it leads. Open it only after you have confirmed the
  vacancy, and check that the page it opens is on the organisation's own address.

## 4. Contact the organisation through a number you found yourself

Use the switchboard or general e-mail on its official website or in a directory, not the contact in the advert. Ask
whether the post is real and how applications should be sent.

## 5. Check the money

A genuine employer does not charge you to apply, to be shortlisted, to be interviewed, for medical tests, for
training, for uniforms or to "secure" a post. Neither does a legitimate recruitment agency. Any request for payment,
mobile-money transfer, airtime or bank or card details before you have a written offer is the clearest sign of a
scam. United Nations agencies state publicly that they never charge fees at any stage of recruitment. [VERIFY: link
the UN's own fraud notice.]

## 6. Check what is being asked for

A CV, a cover letter, certified copies of qualifications and referees are normal. Your bank PIN, one-time codes,
passwords, or originals of your certificates are not. Send copies, never originals, unless you hand them over in
person at a verified office.

## If the advert fails a check

- Do not apply and do not pay.
- On UPNEXT, open the listing and choose **Report this opportunity**; staff read every report.
- If you have already paid or shared bank details, contact your bank or mobile-money provider at once and report it
  to the Zimbabwe Republic Police. [VERIFY: whether a specific cybercrime reporting channel should be named.]

## How UPNEXT helps, and its limits

Every listing on UPNEXT carries a verification label and, under "About this listing", says what the label means and
when the check was made. A label is not a guarantee: always make the checks above for anything you apply to.
