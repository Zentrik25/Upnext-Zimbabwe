Most genuine vacancies in Zimbabwe are advertised in a handful of places, and most fake ones copy the look of those
places. Knowing where real employers publish, and how they word a real notice, saves time and protects you.

## Where employers actually advertise

- **The organisation's own website and recruitment portal.** Banks, mining houses, United Nations agencies,
  international NGOs and large manufacturers usually publish every post on their own careers page or an applicant
  tracking system, and accept applications only there. If a listing elsewhere says "apply online", the online form
  should be on the organisation's own address.
- **Newspapers.** Public bodies, councils, parastatals and many private firms still place notices in the national and
  regional press, often with a postal address and a reference number. [VERIFY: name the main titles editors want to
  mention, if any.]
- **Government and public-service recruitment.** Posts in the civil service are filled through the Public Service
  Commission, and posts in public health through the Health Service Board. [VERIFY: confirm the current recruitment
  route and where each body publishes vacancies.]
- **Local authorities.** Rural district councils and municipalities advertise their own posts, sometimes only by
  notice board, newspaper and a council e-mail address.
- **Recruitment agencies.** An agency may advertise for a client it does not name. A legitimate agency is registered
  [VERIFY: registration requirement for employment agencies under the Labour Act [Chapter 28:01] and its
  regulations], names itself, and never charges the job seeker a placement fee.
- **Social media and messaging groups.** Many real notices are reshared on WhatsApp, Facebook and LinkedIn, usually
  as a photo of the original advert. Treat a reshared image as a lead, not as the source: find the same notice on the
  organisation's own channel before you act on it.

## Search UPNEXT Careers well

- Start from the kind of opportunity: Jobs, Internships & Attachments, Graduate Trainee Programmes, Scholarships,
  Tenders or Grants each have their own list.
- Search by the words employers really use. Zimbabwean notices often say "Graduate Trainee", "Attachment",
  "Artisan", "Clerk", "Officer" or a grade ("Grade 11"), so try those as well as your field.
- Sort by closing date to see what is still open this week.
- Open the listing and read "About this listing": it says when UPNEXT published it, what the verification label
  means and, where one is recorded, the original source.

## Signs of a genuine notice

- It names the organisation, the post, the place of work and a closing date.
- It tells you exactly how to apply: a portal on the organisation's own address, a named e-mail address, or a postal
  or hand-delivery address with a reference number.
- The contact details match what the organisation publishes elsewhere.
- It asks for documents you would expect (CV, certified copies of qualifications, referees) and nothing more.
- It never asks for money at any stage.

A notice that is anonymous ("a leading organisation"), gives only a WhatsApp number or a free webmail address, or
promises unusually high pay for little experience is not necessarily fake, but it needs more checking. Our guide
**How to verify a recruitment advertisement** walks through the checks step by step.

## Keep a simple record

Write down each post you apply for, the date, the reference number and how you applied. Many employers say "only
shortlisted candidates will be contacted"; a record tells you when it is reasonable to assume an application was not
taken forward, and protects you if someone later claims to be calling about it.
